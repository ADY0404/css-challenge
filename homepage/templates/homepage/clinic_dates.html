Services Offered at the Clinic

Our volunteer technicians provide assistance across hardware and software systems:

Linux Dual-Boot & OS

Proper partitioning, GRUB setup, Ubuntu 24.04 LTS / Fedora 40 installations alongside Windows, and EFI boot recovery.

Dev Toolchains & IDEs

Configuration of Docker Desktop, WSL2, Python/Pip/Venv, Node.js, Git & SSH keys, C/C++ GCC compilers, and VS Code.

Hardware Diagnostics

RAM/SSD installation, thermal paste repasting, cooling fan de-dusting, battery cycle health checks, and drive health reports.

Malware & Cleanup

Adware and crypto-miner elimination, background process optimization, drive defragmentation/TRIM, and browser resetting.

What to Bring to Your Session

  • Laptop Charger: Always bring your original power brick and cord. Diagnostic tests and OS installs require uninterrupted power.
  • Data Backup: Back up your coursework, projects, and personal documents to Google Drive, GitHub, or an external drive before your appointment.
  • Empty USB Drive (16GB+): If you are requesting a Linux dual-boot or clean OS installation, please bring a blank flash drive.
  • Account Passwords & BitLocker Keys: Have your Windows Administrator password or BitLocker recovery key ready if disk encryption is active.

No. The PC Clinic is an initiative of the Computer Science Society open to all students and university staff across every department and faculty. Whether you need an operating system install for engineering courses or virus cleaning for general studies, our doors are open.

All diagnostics, installation labor, thermal cleaning, and software installations are 100% free of charge. If your computer requires new hardware parts (such as a larger SSD or additional RAM), you bring the compatible component, and our student technicians will safely install and configure it with you.

For security and accountability, we encourage students to stay with their laptop while our technicians work on it. This also gives you a chance to learn how the diagnostics, terminal commands, or partitioning tools work so you can troubleshoot your machine in the future!

We welcome students who are comfortable with hardware diagnostics, Linux environments, or troubleshooting. Reach out to David Koomson or any member of the CSS Executive Committee, or email clinic@css.community to join our technician volunteer roster.
